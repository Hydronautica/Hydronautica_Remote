The Components view takes the same blade-root loads through every other part (bearings, gears, shaft, bolts) with the standard formulas and puts them all on one scale: how fast each one is using its design life.

Inputs. The global model gives the three blade-root loads, which add up to six loads at the hub centre in the fixed frame, plus the nacelle's accelerations. Gravity is the vertical acceleration case. The bedplate is held at the yaw bearing, and the reaction there is checked against the tower-top load from the global model.

Drivetrain. Shaft and gearbox act as one stiff body tied to the bedplate at the main bearing (radial and axial only, so no torque) and at the two gearbox torque arms (rubber mounts). The rotor torque therefore reaches the bedplate as a push-pull pair at the torque-arm lugs, which is why the lugs light up under Mx.

What is representative. The bedplate shape, wall thickness, spring stiffnesses and load records are illustrative, sized for a 10–15 MW class turbine. The method is the real one: unit solves once, then superposition for any record, then rainflow and S-N at each hot spot.
