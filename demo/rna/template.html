Rotor–nacelle assembly · geared drivetrain, 3-point mount · representative, about 14 MW class

From blade-root loads to every part, by FE

Three finite element models (the hub, the shaft with the gearbox housing, and the bedplate), each solved once for unit loads. What one part's supports carry becomes the load on the next, so the blade-root loads from the global model run down the chain to the tower top, and every part's stress is a sum of its unit fields. The chain is checked against plain statics at the hub and the tower top.

The chain. Hub: held at the shaft flange, loaded by the three blade roots (6 loads each) and its own inertia; it turns with the rotor, so it lives in the rotor frame. Drivetrain: shaft and gearbox housing in one model, joined by the carrier bearing and the planet carrier at the ring gear; held by the main bearing (self-aligning, so no moment) and the two torque-arm elastomers. Bedplate: loaded by those reactions and held at the yaw bearing.

Why it closes. Each model is solved once per unit load and its support reactions are stored as a matrix. Multiplying matrices passes the loads down the chain. At the hub and the tower top the result is compared with rigid-body statics of the global loads; they agree to within 1 %. The differences come from the hub's centre of mass not sitting exactly at the hub centre and from the main bearing's distributed springs.

What is representative. Geometry, wall thicknesses, spring stiffnesses, masses, S-N curves and load records are illustrative for a 14 MW class turbine. The voxel meshes give false stress peaks at supports and load points, so those zones are left out of the hot-spot search; a production model would use a smooth tetrahedral mesh from CAD. Bearings, gears and bolts are contact problems, so they keep their standard formulas, fed by the chain's interface loads.
