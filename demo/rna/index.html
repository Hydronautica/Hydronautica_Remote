Where the unit loads go on each casting

Each cast part gets its own FE model. Every load point on it gets six unit cases: three forces and three moments. The model is held at one support. Pick a part to see its load points, its support and its usual crack locations. Drag to orbit.

Load points and support
–

One support per model. Each unit load goes in at its load point and comes out at the support, so every case is in equilibrium. The global model must give the matching set of applied loads. For the mainframe that is the bearing seats, the generator supports and its own inertia, all reacted at the yaw bearing.

Spread the load like the real part. Each unit load is applied through a distributing connection over the bearing seat, flange or bolt circle, not at one node. The arrows mark the reference point of that connection.

Bearings carry five of the six. A rolling bearing cannot pass torque about the shaft axis, so its Mx case is struck through. Torque reaches the mainframe through the generator stator supports instead.

Rotating loads. Blade-root and hub loads rotate with the rotor. At each time step they are turned into the casting's axes using the rotor azimuth before the unit stresses are combined.
