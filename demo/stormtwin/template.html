How it works

One chain from the sea surface to a bolt

Every turbine is screened over the full storm record. Turbines that are flagged, or that carry a known condition such as scour or low preload, get a deep-dive on their largest wave group.

Buoy, radar or hindcast data at each position, evolved with the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

Loads

Morison loading along the pile, plus a slamming impulse when the crest is breaking.

Beam tower, RNA and blades on a hybrid shell monopile with nonlinear p-y soil springs.

Hot spots

Section forces drive local finite-element models of flange bolts, door frames and welds.

Local FE · S-N

Verdict

Pre-agreed rules turn utilisation and fatigue into a red, amber or green call with actions.

Inspection scope

Global to local: the tower-base flange

The global model gives the bending moment at the flange. The local flange model turns it into the force in each bolt. Below a threshold the joint stays closed and the bolt barely feels the load. Once the gap opens the bolt picks up load fast, through prying, and that is where storm fatigue and preload loss happen. Lost preload moves the curve and makes every later storm worse.

Bolt force against tension per bolt segment. Demo model: Schmidt–Neuper-type trilinear law standing in for the local FE flange segment with contact.

How much to trust each wave model

The same wave height gives different crest kinematics depending on the wave theory. StormTwin reports that spread and flags results outside a model's valid range. From our sweep towards breaking (T = 10 s, d = 100 m):

Wave heightWhat we found
up to 0.4 HbSecond-order NLS and Dysthe agree with the fully nonlinear stream function to about 15% on crest velocity and acceleration.
0.5 to 0.8 HbThe choice of crest extrapolation matters more than the wave theory. Second-order models with Taylor extrapolation run well above the stream function.
0.9 Hb and upEvery focused-wave model predicts the crest breaks (particle speed above wave speed). Use a slamming load model, not finer kinematics.

About this demonstration. The storm, farm, turbine and condition data are synthetic. The turbine is a 15 MW-class monopile design sized loosely after public reference turbines; it is not a real asset. Waves come from NLS simulations of a random sea; loads, global response and hot-spot results come from demo-grade models (Morison loading, a nonlinear beam on API p-y springs, Schmidt–Neuper-type bolt law, stress concentration factors, DNV-RP-C203 and EN 1993-1-9 S-N curves) standing in for the production global and local finite-element models.
