    Inspection scope

    Global to local: the tower-base flange

    The global model gives the bending moment at the flange. The local flange model turns it into the force in each bolt. Below a threshold the joint stays closed and the bolt barely feels the load. Once the gap opens the bolt picks up load fast, through prying, and that is where storm fatigue and preload loss happen. Lost preload moves the curve and makes every later storm worse.

    Bolt force against tension per bolt segment. Demo model: Schmidt–Neuper-type trilinear law standing in for the local FE flange segment with contact.

    How much to trust each wave model

    The same wave height gives different crest kinematics depending on the wave theory. StormTwin reports that spread and flags results outside a model's valid range. From our sweep towards breaking (T = 10 s, d = 100 m):

    Wave heightWhat we found
    up to 0.4 HbSecond-order NLS and Dysthe agree with the fully nonlinear stream function to about 15% on crest velocity and acceleration.
    0.5 to 0.8 HbThe choice of crest extrapolation matters more than the wave theory. Second-order models with Taylor extrapolation run well above the stream function.
    0.9 Hb and upEvery focused-wave model predicts the crest breaks (particle speed above wave speed). Use a slamming load model, not finer kinematics.

    Monitoring inputs

    Natural frequenciesTower and blade modes from accelerometers (operational modal analysis). Foundation stiffness, scour, flange and blade damage show up as shifts once temperature and operation are normalised out.
    Tilt and rotationInclinometers at the interface: permanent tilt after storms against the serviceability limit.
    StrainGauges at the tower base calibrate the virtual sensors that estimate stress where no gauge can go, such as below the seabed.
    Bolt tensionUltrasonic or instrumented bolts feed preload into the flange model, which changes every later storm assessment.
    Damping and mode shapesDamping estimates check the aerodynamic and soil damping in the model; mode-shape changes help locate damage.
    Corrosion, scour, marine growthCathodic-protection potentials, scour sonar and ROV growth surveys update wall thickness, embedment and added mass.

    The farms, storms, turbines and condition data are synthetic. The turbines are 15 MW- and 10 MW-class monopile designs sized loosely after public reference turbines; they are not real assets. Waves come from NLS simulations of a random sea; loads, global response and hot-spot results come from demo-grade models (Morison loading, a nonlinear beam on API p-y springs, Schmidt–Neuper-type bolt law, stress concentration factors, DNV-RP-C203 and EN 1993-1-9 S-N curves) standing in for the production global and local finite-element models.
